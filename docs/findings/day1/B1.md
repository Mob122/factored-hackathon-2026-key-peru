# B1 - Failure demand while a complaint is open

Generated by `ml/analysis/day1/B1_failure_demand.py` on 2026-09-28 over `ml/data/02_intermediate/` (67,095 complaints, 686,296 call-center interactions). Observation period 2023-06-17 to 2026-06-17 inclusive. Intervals for rate ratios are 95% customer-cluster Poisson bootstraps (1000 replicates); proportions use Wilson 95% intervals; p-values are chi-square tests unless stated. Contacts are counted at customer level (B4: no complaint-to-interaction link exists), always against a baseline.

## Hypothesis

There is measurable failure demand: customers contact the call center more between a complaint's `creation_date` and its closing than they do when no complaint is open, those extra contacts look like follow-ups (`contact_reason`, `requires_followup`), and the excess grows with `resolution_days`. If it fails: Track B is dropped.

## Verdict: **refuted as material: no excess overall; the only real signal is a small bump (1.17x, about 1.1 extra contacts per 100 complaints) in complaints that actually got resolved or closed, fading within two weeks**

- **Overall, the excess is noise.** Main definition, 32,231 complaints with a clean equal-length placebo window: 22,648 contacts inside open windows vs 22,297 in the placebo windows, rate ratio 1.016 [0.996, 1.034], i.e. +1.1 contacts per 100 complaints. Per customer-day, the 54,127 customers with a complaint contact at 4.19 per 1,000 days with an open complaint vs 4.16 without one (pooled 1.008 [1.000, 1.016], month-stratified 1.008); customers with no complaint sit at 4.17.
- **One real but small signal: complaints that actually ended.** For the 13,084 matched complaints with a closing or resolution date (mean 16-day window) the ratio is 1.169 [1.071, 1.299], i.e. +142 contacts (+1.09 per 100 complaints, 0.02% of all interactions). The same-length windows right before creation and right after the end hold 888 and 821 contacts vs 984 inside. By fixed horizon the bump is 491 vs 423 contacts in the first 7 days (1.161 [1.004, 1.321]) and fades by 30 days; never-ended complaints show nothing (0.960 [0.893, 1.040] at 7 days). This subgroup was found after looking at the data, and the lift stays under the 1.2x bar, so it is not failure demand at a scale Track B can use.
- **Contacts during open complaints look like any other contacts.** The `contact_reason` mix matches the placebo windows (p 0.4912); 'Queja' is 16.91% of contacts inside open windows vs 17.24% in placebo. `requires_followup` is 34.40% vs 34.94% (p 0.2240), and 34.83% over all interactions.
- **Volume grows with `resolution_days` only because the window is longer.** Real contacts rise by 0.0044 per extra day and placebo contacts by 0.0041; the excess slope is +0.0003 (SE 0.0004) contacts per day of resolution.
- **Robust to the end-date rule.** The literal rule (end = `closing_date`, else 2026-06-17) gives a matched ratio of 1.012 [0.994, 1.029] over 24,993 complaints and a month-stratified per-customer-day ratio of 1.007.

## Defining the open window

`closing_date` is filled for 2,481 complaints (3.70%) only. Another 13,006 have `resolution_date` but no `closing_date`, and 51,608 (76.92%) have neither, including 34,343 created more than a year before the data end: statuses Open / In Process / Escalated never age out. Under the literal rule every resolved complaint and every stale open one stays open until the data end, so windows run for hundreds of days.

Long stale windows rarely have one, so the matched set leans toward short, resolved complaints; the customer-day view (b) uses every complaint.

## Consequences

- **Failure demand, as B1 defines it, is not in this data at a usable scale.** Customers call at their usual rate whether or not a complaint is open, the calls look the same, and slow resolutions do not generate extra calls beyond the longer window. The one detectable effect, about 1 extra contact per 100 resolved/closed complaints in the first two weeks, is ~0.02% of call volume and starts slightly before creation, so it may just as well be contact that gets a complaint resolved as contact caused by it. By the hypothesis plan's 'Si falla' rule, **Track B loses its central premise**: a 'reduce repeat contacts by resolving faster' story would have no measured effect to point to.
- **Contacts are drawn almost independently of complaints.** Together with B4 (no link) and B3 (no history effect), complaints and interactions behave like nearly independent streams joined only by `customer_id`. Any model that uses one to predict the other will learn base rates.
- **Status and end dates are not trustworthy lifecycle data.** 77% of complaints never close, `closing_date` exists only for 'Closed', and `resolution_days` is uniform on 1-30. Any open-case backlog KPI must declare how it handles this.
