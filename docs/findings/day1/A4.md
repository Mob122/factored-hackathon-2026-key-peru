# A4 - recurrence and temporal structure per customer

Generated by `ml/analysis/day1/A4_recurrence_predictability.py` on 2026-09-28 over `ml/data/02_intermediate/transactions.parquet` (4,425,008 transactions, 2023-06-17 to 2026-06-18). Recurrence uses the 1,029,234 rows with a `merchant_name` (all of type Purchase (1,029,234)). Rates carry Wilson 95% CIs; baselines are the mean of 5 seeds. Predictability uses a temporal holdout = last 6 months (`transaction_date >= 2025-12-18`).

## Hypothesis

Customers have recurring charges (inter-arrival near 30 days, low variance, similar amount per `product_id` and merchant) and a predictable next event, which would justify a sequence model. 'Si falla': drop the sequence model and keep LightGBM on aggregates.

## Verdict: **refuted: no recurrence and no customer-level sequence structure**

- **No billing cycle.** Consecutive product x merchant gaps are flat: monthly peak ratio 1.03 (shuffled 0.97); customer x merchant 1.02 (shuffled 1.00). Only 6.15% of merchant purchases belong to a product x merchant series with >= 3 purchases.
- **Recurring share = chance.** Monthly pairs 0.066% of merchant purchases vs 0.071% shuffled (z = -2.0); monthly series 3 purchases (0.000%) vs 0.001% shuffled. Rules above baseline: none.
- **Amounts carry no merchant identity.** Every merchant, bill-like ones included, has the same amount_usd distribution (mean ~108, CV ~1.45); consecutive same-merchant amounts are within 10% in 10.18% of pairs vs 10.20% with amounts permuted within the card. Bill-like merchants monthly pair 0.077% vs others 0.062%.
- **Next category: (a) does not even beat (b).** Holdout accuracy (a) customer mode 21.09% vs (b) global mode 24.91% (-3.83 pp [-4.00, -3.66]; labels permuted -3.83 pp); (c) previous category 17.97%; log-loss gain -5.00%. A customer's history is a noisy sample of the global mix, so the per-customer mode overfits.
- **Next transaction_type: the signal is the product portfolio, not the customer.** (a) 34.54% vs (b) 24.41% (+10.13 pp, log-loss gain +9.90%), but permuting labels within product_type keeps (a) at 34.50% (beyond portfolio +0.04 pp). Cards emit purchases, accounts emit transfers / withdrawals / deposits; order adds nothing ((c) previous type 27.33% < (a)).
- **Consequence (from 'Si falla'):** drop the sequence model. Merchant, date, amount and category look drawn i.i.d. per row given the product type, so a per-customer next-event model has nothing to learn beyond which products the customer holds. Keep LightGBM on aggregates (product-portfolio features carry the only signal found) and benchmark it against the global and product_type marginals.
