# B3 - Regulator channel and breached SLAs

Generated by `ml/analysis/day1/B3_regulator_sla.py` on 2026-09-28 over `ml/data/02_intermediate/` (67,095 complaints, 2023-06-17 to 2026-06-18). Country comes from `customers.country` (0 complaints without a match). Intervals are Wilson 95%; p-values are chi-square tests of independence. Windows are in 24-hour units from `creation_date`.

## Hypothesis

There is volume in the Regulator reception channel and in `sla_breached`, and the temporal order with earlier contacts carries signal: Regulator complaints are preceded by earlier complaints or contacts from the same customer, especially complaints whose SLA was breached. If it fails: only the SLA risk is used.

## Verdict: **volume confirmed, structure refuted: Regulator and SLA-breach volume exist, but neither depends on country, category or customer history**

- **Volume exists but is small for Regulator.** 717 Regulator complaints (1.07% of all, about 20 per month). 13,495 complaints (20.11%) have `sla_breached = true`. The Regulator breach rate is 22.45% [19.55, 25.65] against 20.09% for the other channels (p 0.1158).
- **No structure by country, category or channel.** The Regulator share does not depend on country (p 0.6198) or category (p 0.6103), and the breach rate does not depend on channel (p 0.4905), country (p 0.2620) or category (p 0.7586).
- **Regulator complaints are not preceded by more history.** Within 90 days before, 31.07% [27.32, 35.08] of Regulator complaints have an earlier complaint or contact from the same customer, versus 33.82% [33.41, 34.24] of non-Regulator complaints (p 0.1766) and 33.64% in the placebo window shifted 180 days back (lift 0.92x).
- **An earlier breached SLA does not lead to a Regulator complaint.** 0.03% [0.01, 0.08] of breached complaints are followed by a Regulator complaint from the same customer within 90 days, versus 0.03% [0.02, 0.05] of non-breached ones (risk ratio 1.14 [0.37, 3.46], p 0.8191). Only 18 such follow-ups exist because repeat complaints are rare, so this test alone cannot exclude a moderate effect. The backward view agrees: a complaint whose customer had a breached complaint in the previous 90 days is Regulator 0.99% [0.39, 2.52], the same as the 1.07% base rate.
- **`sla_breached` looks like a random ~20% flag.** It does not rise with `resolution_days` (corr -0.013, p 0.1845), time to first response (p 0.2245), status (p 0.0842) or priority (p 0.4608). The fallback 'only the SLA risk is used' therefore has no learnable target in `sla_breached` either. An SLA target would have to be derived from timestamps (e.g. `resolution_days` against a declared threshold).

## Consequences

- **Regulator risk is not predictable from what this data holds.** Its share is flat across country and category and unrelated to the customer's prior complaints, contacts or breaches. A Regulator-escalation model would learn the base rate.
- **The 'Si falla' fallback (SLA risk only) does not hold up either.** `sla_breached` does not follow resolution time, first response, priority or status, so a model trained on it has no signal to find. If Track B keeps an SLA angle, define the target from timestamps (`resolution_days`, `first_response_date`) against a declared threshold and say that the dataset's own flag was not used.
- **The Regulator and SLA fields can still be shown as descriptive KPIs** (volume, share, trend) in the product, as long as they are not presented as drivers.
